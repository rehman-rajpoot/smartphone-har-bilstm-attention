# Experimental & Architectural Analysis

## Subject-Disjoint Validation vs. Random Window Splitting
Continuous 50 Hz inertial streams in the UCI HAR dataset are segmented into 2.56-second windows (128 samples) with a **50% stride overlap** (64 shared samples, or 1.28 seconds, between adjacent windows). Randomly partitioning windows into training and validation subsets would place half of nearly every validation window inside a neighbouring training window from the exact same physical stride or posture segment. Furthermore, individual volunteers exhibit distinct gait cadences, step-impact amplitudes, and waist-belt mounting angles. Splitting strictly by subject (holding out volunteers 3, 15, 25, and 30 for validation and evaluating on 9 unseen test volunteers) eliminates window-overlap leakage and measures true cross-person generalisation.

## Stationary Posture Ambiguity: Sitting vs. Standing
**SITTING** and **STANDING** represent the dominant source of classification errors: 77 sitting windows are predicted as standing and 85 standing windows are predicted as sitting (162 errors in total, or **59.6%** of all 272 test-set misclassifications). In both postures, dynamic linear acceleration (`body_acc_x/y/z`) and angular velocity (`body_gyro_x/y/z`) stay near zero aside from subtle breathing or postural sway. Because the smartphone is worn on the **waist** rather than on the thigh, the torso remains upright in both sitting and standing, leaving only slight pelvic-tilt variations across `total_acc_*` that overlap substantially with person-to-person belt placement differences.

## Gravity Vector Signature in Laying Detection
**`total_acc_x`** (paired with the corresponding redistribution onto **`total_acc_y`** and **`total_acc_z`**) makes **LAYING** straightforward to separate from every upright activity. While `body_acc_*` removes gravity via high-pass filtering, `total_acc_*` preserves the $1g$ gravitational acceleration vector in the phone's body frame. Across all five upright activities, the phone's $x$-axis aligns vertically with the torso (`total_acc_x` $\approx +0.95g$ to $+1.00g$). Lying horizontally rotates the sensor frame by roughly $90^\circ$, causing `total_acc_x` to drop near $0.07g$ while shifting gravity onto the transverse $y$ and $z$ axes.

## Temporal Attention Concentration: Dynamic Locomotion vs. Static Postures
Learned attention weights $\alpha_t$ are **sharply peaked during moving activities** and nearly flat during static postures. Compared against a uniform baseline ($1/128 \approx 0.0078$), the mean peak weight $\max_t \alpha_t$ reaches **0.2792** for `WALKING` ($35.7\times$ uniform), **0.2707** for `WALKING_DOWNSTAIRS` ($34.6\times$ uniform), and **0.1550** for `WALKING_UPSTAIRS` ($19.8\times$ uniform), compared with **0.0110** for `LAYING` ($1.4\times$ uniform), **0.0152** for `SITTING` ($1.9\times$ uniform), and **0.0164** for `STANDING` ($2.1\times$ uniform). Dynamic locomotion concentrates discriminative biomechanical cues into brief heel-strike impacts and push-off reversals, whereas static postures maintain a constant gravity orientation throughout the entire 2.56-second buffer.

## Offline Window Buffering and Bidirectional Sequence Processing
A bidirectional LSTM is appropriate in this pipeline because inference runs on **complete, buffered 2.56-second windows**: all 128 samples $(x_1, \dots, x_{128})$ are already recorded before the classifier emits a prediction for that window. Concatenating forward and backward hidden states $h_t = [\overrightarrow{h}_t; \overleftarrow{h}_t]$ lets each time step incorporate full gait-cycle context from both directions without leaking future windows. By contrast, bidirectional recurrence cannot be used in causal sample-by-sample streaming tasks—such as next-step time-series forecasting, zero-latency pre-impact fall alarms, or closed-loop control—where future samples $x_{t+1}, \dots, x_T$ have not yet arrived.

## Additive Cell-State Dynamics and Gradient Norm Clipping
In a standard RNN, $h_t = \tanh(W x_t + U h_{t-1} + b)$, backpropagating through 128 time steps repeatedly multiplies gradients by $\text{diag}(1 - h_k^2)\, U^\top$, causing exponential **vanishing gradients** when singular values fall below unity or $\tanh$ saturates. An LSTM overcomes this bottleneck via its additive cell-state update:
$$c_t = f_t \odot c_{t-1} + i_t \odot g_t$$
Because the direct cell-to-cell Jacobian $\partial c_t / \partial c_{t-1} = \text{diag}(f_t)$ acts element-wise through the forget gate, setting $f_t \approx 1$ creates an unimpeded gradient highway across 128 steps. Complementarily, **gradient norm clipping** (`clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), 1.0)`) guards against **exploding gradients** by rescaling occasional high-curvature gradient spikes back to unit $\ell_2$ norm before Adam updates the weights.
